Building Facade Classifier

Unsupervised computer vision pipeline that sorts building façades into three architectural categories — industrial, vernacular and transitional — using handcrafted geometric features rather than a trained classifier. It runs over a 400-image dataset, with every stage from edge detection to clustering to evaluation implemented from scratch on top of NumPy. A short accompanying report covers the mathematical apparatus and evaluation in full.

Stack

Python 3, NumPy, Pillow, Matplotlib for visualization. No OpenCV, scikit-learn or other CV/ML libraries — convolution, symmetry search, PCA, k-means and the clustering metrics are all hand-implemented, in order to build understanding of the underlying mathematics rather than calling into a pre-built method.

Approach

The pipeline is deliberately split into five explicit, interpretable stages rather than a single end-to-end black box:

  1. mathematical analysis of the façade images (grayscale conversion, gradient-based edge extraction)
  2. construction of an interpretable geometric feature space
  3. dimensionality reduction via PCA to inspect the intrinsic structure of that feature space
  4. unsupervised k-means clustering into three groups
  5. assignment of new, unseen samples to the learned clusters, with a confidence score

Feature engineering

Each image is converted to grayscale and contrast-normalized (median/MAD centering through a soft tanh squash) to reduce sensitivity to illumination. Spatial gradients Gx, Gy are computed with a hand-written Sobel convolution, giving an edge magnitude map that emphasizes structural contours and repetitive architectural elements such as window grids and cornices:

$$E(x,y) = \sqrt{G_x(x,y)^2 + G_y(x,y)^2}$$

Reflection symmetry is scored by brute-force searching candidate axes, parameterized by angle θ and offset ρ, over a range of angles and offsets. For each axis hypothesis the edge map is reflected with bilinear sampling to obtain (x',y') and compared against the original:

$$S_{ref} = \frac{\sum E(x,y)\,E(x',y')}{\sum E(x,y)^2}$$

the maximum score across all tested axes is kept as the final symmetry measure. A weighting mask discards the top and bottom margins of the image so sky and ground don't bias the axis search toward the façade's silhouette.

Periodicity is measured separately along both axes by projecting the edge map onto one-dimensional profiles,

$$p_x(x) = \sum_y E(x,y), \qquad p_y(y) = \sum_x E(x,y)$$

then taking the strongest peak of each profile's autocorrelation within a predefined lag range:

$$S_{per} = \max_{\ell \in [\ell_{\min},\, \ell_{\max}]} \operatorname{corr}(p,\, p_\ell)$$

which captures the repetition of window bays horizontally and floors vertically. Edge density rounds this out as the share of pixels exceeding a fixed edge-magnitude threshold τ,

$$D_{edge} = \frac{1}{HW}\sum_{x,y} \mathbb{1}_{E(x,y) > \tau}$$

alongside a horizontal/vertical anisotropy scalar A. Together, every façade collapses into a compact 5-dimensional feature vector:

$$\mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} S_{ref} & S_{per}^{x} & S_{per}^{y} & D_{edge} & A \end{bmatrix}^{\top}$$

Clustering & assignment

The five features are z-score standardized on the training set,

$$\mathbf{x}' = \frac{\mathbf{x} - \boldsymbol{\mu}}{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}$$

optionally reduced to two dimensions with a from-scratch, covariance-based PCA — eigen-decomposing the covariance matrix of the standardized feature matrix X,

$$\mathbf{C} = \frac{1}{N-1}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}$$

and projecting onto the leading eigenvectors — then clustered with a hand-implemented k-means (k=3, up to 300 iterations) that minimizes the within-cluster sum of squared distances:

$$\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_i \in C_k} \lVert \mathbf{x}_i - \boldsymbol{\mu}_k \rVert^2$$

Rather than hardcoding which cluster is which, each cluster's centroid μk is read back in standardized feature space and labelled automatically from its most distinctive dimensions — industrial façades trending toward strong periodicity and geometric regularity, vernacular ones toward greater irregularity and contextual variation. At inference, a new image goes through the same feature extraction, is standardized with the saved training mean/scale, optionally reprojected through the saved PCA components, and assigned to its nearest centroid. Assignment certainty is reported as a distance-based confidence score,

$$\text{conf} = 1 - \frac{d_1}{d_1+d_2}$$

the relative margin between the distances d1 and d2 to the nearest and second-nearest centroid — values close to 1 indicate an unambiguous match, values near 0.5 flag a transitional façade sitting between two categories.

Results & evaluation

Cluster quality is checked with a from-scratch Silhouette coefficient, scoring how well each sample fits its own cluster relative to the next-closest one:

$$s(i) = \frac{b(i) - a(i)}{\max\{a(i),\, b(i)\}}$$

where a(i) is the mean distance from sample i to the rest of its own cluster and b(i) the mean distance to the nearest other cluster; the reported score averages s(i) over all samples. The Davies–Bouldin index instead balances intra-cluster compactness against inter-cluster separation. For each cluster's scatter

$$S_k = \frac{1}{|C_k|}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_i \in C_k} \lVert \mathbf{x}_i - \boldsymbol{\mu}_k \rVert$$

the similarity between two clusters is

$$R_{kj} = \frac{S_k + S_j}{\lVert \boldsymbol{\mu}_k - \boldsymbol{\mu}_j \rVert}$$

and the index averages each cluster's worst-case similarity to any other cluster — lower is better:

$$\text{DB} = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K} \max_{j \neq k} R_{kj}$$

Finally, the PCA explained-variance ratio of each retained component,

$$\text{EVR}_m = \frac{\lambda_m}{\sum_r \lambda_r}$$

quantifies how much of the feature space's structure survives the 2-D projection shown above. The full set of results:

Taken together, the metrics point to a balanced trade-off between cluster separation and architectural variability — moderate Silhouette/Davies–Bouldin scores are expected given real façades sit on a continuum rather than in three hard buckets, while the confidence distribution (mean 0.80, median 0.83) shows most assignments are unambiguous, with the lower P10 tail marking the transitional cases visible as overlap in the PCA plot above.

Running the project

The pipeline is split into four standalone scripts. symmetry_features.py scans a local image directory and writes a features.csv. train_cluster_model.py fits standardization, PCA and k-means on that CSV and saves a cluster_model.npz. predict_cluster.py loads that model and assigns new images to the nearest center. metrics.py is run optionally afterward to report Silhouette, Davies–Bouldin and confidence statistics for the resulting clustering.
