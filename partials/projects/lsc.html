Language Specific Correction for Romanian

Grammatical error correction system for Romanian that tags each word directly with its correction, rather than generating a rewritten sentence with a seq2seq model. Every source token is labelled with an edit tag of the form error-type_word, so the model only has to classify each token instead of generating free text. The approach is adapted from a method built for English (GECToR) and re-derives its own tag vocabulary and error taxonomy for Romanian from scratch.

Stack

Python, PyTorch, Hugging Face Transformers, a pretrained dumitrescustefan/bert-base-romanian-cased-v1 encoder, a Romanian fork of ERRANT for error type extraction, and a Romanian MSD lexicon for morphosyntactic tagging.

Dataset

Roughly 1.18 million source/target sentence pairs, combined from five corpora — RoTexts, CNA, RoComments, the Leipzig Corpora and Romanian parliamentary debates — each contributing a different mix of naturally-occurring and synthetically-corrupted errors, plus a share of already-correct identity pairs so the model also sees well-formed Romanian during training. A synthetic corruption step keeps the error distribution statistically realistic rather than relying solely on naturally-occurring mistakes.

Tag annotation

Each source/target pair is run through ERRANT to obtain a first pass of error-type labels. Where ERRANT can't classify an edit, it falls back to |||OTHER|||, so a Romanian morphosyntactic lexicon is used as a second signal: comparing a word's tag in its source form against its target form (e.g. Vmip3s vs. Vmip2s) maps the mismatch onto an ERRANT-style category such as R:VERB:SVA, catching subject-verb agreement, plural and word-order errors that ERRANT alone misses. Combining both signals into error-type_word tags produces 3,702 classes, well down from the ~5,000 of the English system it's adapted from. The distribution is heavily skewed towards spelling and diacritics (R:SPELL alone accounts for ~59% of all edits), so training uses weighted cross-entropy loss with square-root inverse-frequency class weights to keep the rarer grammatical categories from being drowned out.

Pipeline

Training

The tagger is a token classification head on top of the pretrained Romanian BERT encoder, fine-tuned for up to 10 epochs with early stopping (patience 3) on validation F0.5. The first 2 epochs are run "cold", with the encoder frozen and only the classification head trained at a higher learning rate, before unfreezing the encoder for the remaining "warm" epochs at a lower rate. Training uses mixed-precision, gradient clipping, a linear warmup/decay schedule and the weighted loss described above; validation runs on roughly 10,000 held-out sentences from the combined dataset.

Inference

At inference time, the tagger predicts one tag per token together with its confidence. Predictions below a confidence threshold (0.7–0.8 depending on the script) are discarded in favor of $KEEP, so the model only edits words it's actually confident about. Edits are applied — keeping, deleting, appending after, or replacing a token depending on the tag — and the corrected sentence is fed back through the tagger for up to three passes, stopping early as soon as a pass produces no further change.

Performance

On the combined test set, the system reaches an F1 of 0.8045 (precision 0.8301, recall 0.7804) at a test loss of 0.1432. Evaluated per source dataset with the standard GEC F0.5 metric across iterations, it scores up to 0.66 on RoComments, 0.53 on RoTexts and 0.42 on CNA — beating the current state of the art on RoComments across all iterations.
